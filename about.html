About the toolkit

Who we are, and who this toolkit is built for.

The CE4CSID Toolkit is a practical, open access resource for anyone planning, running, monitoring, or evaluating community engagement in Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease work. It was written for people with no prior engagement experience as much as for specialists.

The working group

Research with communities, not research on them.

The CE4CSID Working Group was established during the first Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network Annual General Meeting, held from 21 to 25 July 2025 in Bangkok and Kanchanaburi, Thailand. It brings together researchers and practitioners who saw the same gap in their own work: a growing demand for community level engagement in CSID research, and very little practical guidance on how to do it well.

Data from communities feeds climate sensitive infectious disease models, research, and digital tools. Yet communities are often excluded from research design and decision making, have limited access to what the study finds, and may never hear about the tools built from their contributions. Many CSID initiatives operate at a high scientific level with community participation largely limited to data collection.

That disconnect risks findings, interventions, and tools that do not reflect local realities, priorities, or needs. It can undermine trust, limit uptake, and keep communities from using scientific knowledge to strengthen prevention and resilience.

The gap we are closing. Existing community engagement toolkits are often too technical, lack adaptable templates, reflect Global North contexts, or offer no support through implementation. This toolkit was built to answer those four problems directly.
What is inside

Eight practical elements, one research cycle.

The toolkit follows a project from the earliest stages of conceptualisation through to dissemination of findings and evaluation of outcomes.

Community engagement roadmap

Step by step guidance for planning, implementing, monitoring, and evaluating engagement programmes.

Resource selection flowchart

Guidance to help you identify appropriate engagement activities, communication approaches, and multimedia resources.

Community engagement checklist

A working checklist to make sure the essentials are considered and addressed throughout the research process.

Ready to use templates

Adaptable resources for proposals, ethics submissions, engagement activities, monitoring and evaluation, graphic design, risk communication, and dissemination.

Ethical and inclusive guidance

Support for meaningful participation, respect for community knowledge, accessibility, and responsible research practice.

Environmental considerations

How to integrate environmentally sustainable practice into engagement activities and logistics.

Responsible data use

Practical considerations for ethical data collection, management, communication, and use.

Experiences and lessons

Case studies and insight from applying engagement approaches in different research settings.

Who this is for

Built for people doing CSID work, at any level of experience.

The toolkit is primarily for CSID researchers, policymakers, public health professionals, and practitioners. It is not limited to Network members, and prior community engagement experience is not required.

Researchers

Designing and running CSID studies who want engagement built into the methodology from the start.

Programme implementers

Turning findings into interventions, tools, and services that communities are expected to adopt and trust.

Policy makers

Shaping CSID policy who need a credible record of community perspective behind their decisions.

Pilot sites

Tested in two settings, built to keep changing.

Community engagement cannot be applied the same way everywhere. The initiative runs two pilot use cases in distinct cultural and socioeconomic settings, one in Asia and one in Africa, to assess how relevant, accessible, usable, and adaptable the toolkit is in real research environments, including resource limited settings.

What the pilots teach will shape later versions, and the toolkit is meant to stay a living resource that responds to emerging evidence and to feedback from researchers, practitioners, and communities.

Support

A helpdesk behind the documents.

A dedicated helpdesk supports researchers and practitioners adapting the toolkit to their own context. Use it for advice on engagement approaches, communication strategy, culturally appropriate wording, graphics, and the practical application of toolkit resources.

The helpdesk also supports knowledge sharing across the CSID Network and feeds user experience back into the next version of the toolkit.

Intended impact

From conducting research on communities to conducting research with them.

Community members are not sources of data or recipients of findings. They are partners in identifying problems, shaping research priorities, developing interventions, and applying what the research learns.

Stronger participation

Accessible, accurate, adaptable resources that make meaningful participation easier to plan and sustain.

Trust and uptake

Knowledge co-production and prompt, culturally appropriate communication that improve trust in research and uptake of interventions.

Earlier detection

Better risk communication, earlier detection of disease risks, stronger prevention, and more resilient communities.
